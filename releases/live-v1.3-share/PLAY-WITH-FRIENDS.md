# THE SURVIVAL GAME — how to play with friends

Co-op takes **one download total**. Everybody else just uses a web browser.

## The host (one person)

1. Download the game for your system:
   - Windows: `TheSurvivalGame.exe`
   - Linux: `TheSurvivalGame-linux`
   - Mac (Apple Silicon): `TheSurvivalGame-mac.zip` — unzip it and run
     TheSurvivalGame.app inside.
2. Run it. Press **START**.
3. The menu shows your join address, like `http://192.168.1.42:8379`.
   That's it — you're hosting. The game itself serves the web version
   and runs the session; there is no separate server to start and no
   mode to pick.

## If your OS warns you about the game

The game isn't code-signed (signing certificates are a yearly fee), so
Windows and macOS show a scary warning the first time you run a
download. The downloads are the same game as the browser version above.

- **Windows:** on "Windows protected your PC", click **More info** →
  **Run anyway**. If Defender removes the file instead, open Windows
  Security → Protection history, find TheSurvivalGame.exe and choose
  Restore / Allow on device.
- **Mac:** don't double-click the first time — **right-click (or
  Control-click) the app → Open → Open**. After that it launches
  normally.
- **Android:** when installing the APK, allow installs from your
  browser/files app when Android asks.

## Everyone else (up to 3 more players)

1. Be on the same network/Wi-Fi as the host.
2. Open the host's address in any browser — `http://192.168.1.42:8379`
   (whatever address the host's menu shows). Phones and tablets work too.
3. You drop straight into the host's game. No install, no account,
   nothing to choose.

## Save slots

- You get **three save slots**. START asks which slot the new game
  lives in; CONTINUE asks which save to load — each card shows whose
  run it is, their heaviest hat, kills and time. Starting over an
  occupied slot asks first, so nothing is erased by accident.
- **Pause is a menu now:** CONTINUE resumes, and **SAVE + QUIT TO
  title** banks the run. Loading a save always resumes at the
  **centre of the Bug Mall**, wherever you were when you saved —
  a safe place to get your bearings (and your shopping done).
  TITLE** files the run into its slot and drops you back at the
  title screen. (Saves from older versions land in slot 1
  automatically.)

## Local co-op — two controllers, one screen, one couch

Plug in **two controllers** (or pair two Bluetooth gamepads) and a
**2P CO-OP** row appears on the title screen all by itself. Turn it
on, then START or CONTINUE as usual:

- Player 1 uses the keyboard or the first controller; player 2 uses
  the second controller. P2 drops in beside P1 — in a fresh run, or
  by pressing any button on pad 2 mid-run.
- The pair shares **one save slot**, just like a solo run: P2's
  weapons, upgrades and hat are saved and restored with the run.
- Stay close and you share one screen. Wander apart and the screen
  **splits automatically** — gradually, as you separate: the second
  view peels open from the side your partner is drifting toward,
  side-by-side when you're apart east-west, stacked when you're
  apart north-south, and it sweeps shut again as you come back
  together. It always settles at an **even** split — the seam comes
  to rest exactly in the middle, never lopsided. It also splits
  whenever either of you steps into the mall, so the shopper
  browses in peace (shield up, hat on the counter) while the other
  keeps squashing. Each view carries the HUD that fits it — a small
  name-and-health strip while a view is still opening, the full
  card once there's room.
- Each of you has your own hat, weapons and store cursor. Either
  player's Start pauses. If one of you goes down, the split ends
  **that instant** — one shared screen again — and a **red arrow**
  points the standing player to the body. Get there, stand close,
  and press Interact to **revive them with carcasses off your own
  hat**: the price follows the downed player's capability (their
  best weapon and health), so a fresh partner is cheap to save and
  a stacked one takes a real haul. They come back at half health.
- Works in the Windows, Linux and browser builds with any two
  standard gamepads. (Mac build: controllers aren't wired up yet —
  keyboard P1 only for now.)

## Kindle Fire & Fire TV

- **Fire tablet:** open this page in the Silk browser, download
  `TheSurvivalGame.apk`, and allow the install when Fire OS asks —
  the full touch game, offline. Bluetooth gamepads pair too, so
  local co-op works on the tablet.
- **Fire TV / Fire TV Stick:** pair a Bluetooth gamepad first
  (Settings → Controllers & Bluetooth), then sideload the same APK
  (the free "Downloader" app can fetch it from this page). It now
  shows up on the Fire TV home screen like a proper TV app. The
  remote's D-pad + Select can steer menus and movement in a pinch,
  but a gamepad is the way to play.

## Controls

- **Move:** WASD or arrow keys — you face and fire the way you move.
- **Fire:** SPACE. **Interact / enter store:** E. **Build a wall:** F (costs 3 carcasses).
- **Pause (solo):** P or Esc.
- **Rebind anything:** the SETTINGS screen on the main menu lets you put
  every action on the key you want; your bindings are saved.
- **Gamepad:** left stick or D-pad moves, A fires, B interacts, X builds
  a wall, bumpers switch weapons, LT sprints, Start pauses. The moment
  a controller is connected the game switches to pad mode on its own:
  the on-screen hints show pad buttons, the touch buttons disappear,
  and the keyboard-only SETTINGS row leaves the menu until you unplug.
- **On mobile:** two control schemes — tap **CONTROLS** on the menu to
  switch (the choice is saved):
  - **Drag:** drag to move + shoot. Tap to select in menus; walk up to
    the Bug Bounty store and tap the store itself to go in, tap a row
    to select it, tap it again to buy, tap outside the panel to leave.
  - **Stick:** a floating joystick appears wherever your left thumb
    lands and follows it — push to move. **FIRE** shoots, **USE**
    enters/leaves the store, **WPN** switches weapons. Menus and the
    store still work by tapping.
  - Both schemes have a **WALL** button: it throws down 3 carcasses
    from your hat as a defensive wall (it pulses when you can afford
    one). Walls block bugs — they have to stop and chew through — but
    your shots fly right over them, so a wall line is a firing line:
    stand behind it and shoot.
- **Zen mode:** toggle **ZEN MODE** on the menu for a calmer game —
  every bug is gigantic and very slow. Zen runs don't set records.
- **Stack your weapons:** buy the same weapon again (up to 5 copies)
  and every shot fans out — two copies fire at two angles, three at
  three, spread across your front. Each copy costs more than the one
  before (copy N costs N× the base price). Stacks are UNCAPPED — go as
  high as you can afford. The Debugger stacks differently: each copy
  adds another beam, spaced evenly around you — two copies zap front
  and back, three cover every 120°, five every 72° — and every copy
  also makes the beams reach a little farther. Upgrades stack
  forever too — Overclock, Caffeine, Shield, Hoodie, Espresso, all
  of them, with prices that keep climbing past the old menu. The
  store shows every stack as a plain number (X1, X2, X3...), and the
  weapon chips under your status card (or the WPN button, or keys
  1–5) switch between the weapons you own.
- THREAT: the pill at the top of the screen shows the world's THREAT
  level, computed from your damage per second and your health and
  defenses. It starts at ×1.0 and climbs as you upgrade — bigger,
  faster, more numerous bugs everywhere, even at the Epicenter. The
  game keeps up with you, forever.
- THE HAT: it doesn't stop growing. Past a few dozen carcasses it
  spreads wider and wider instead of just taller, and the camera
  zooms out as it grows so you can still see your kingdom.
- **Bragging rights:** after a game over (browser and Android app),
  buttons under the game let you share your run — a score-card photo
  with your heaviest hat, kills and time, plus a link to the game —
  save the photo, copy the text, or share straight to Facebook and
  friends.

## The Bug Mall, buddies, bowling & Beethoven

- **Difficulty:** the title menu now has a DIFFICULTY row. EASY is the
  classic game; MEDIUM, HARD and BUGAGGEDON only make the bugs meaner.
- **The Bug Mall:** the hut at the Epicenter is a walk-in mall now.
  Inside: the **Bug Bounty** counter (the store, same as always), the
  **Bug Bank** — deposit carcasses to keep them safe
  from bites (banked bugs weigh nothing, but they never count toward
  your heaviest hat — the bank moves **all, half or a quarter** of a
  stack at a time, both ways), **Banzai Buddies**, and the
  **Bug Bowl** lanes.
- **Banzai Buddies:** adopt a puppy (100 kg), a kitty (150 kg), a
  moose (300 kg), a platypus (200 kg), a kitchen sink (175 kg) — or
  a **pet rock** (60 kg) — a flat-bottomed little crag with a jagged,
  hilly top that gets around by flopping end over end, is very slow,
  and almost never faints. You only get **one**. Adopting one is a
  little ceremony: pick the species, then a **name** from sixty
  Elves out of *The Silmarillion*, *The Hobbit* and *The Lord of
  the Rings*, then a **coat** — eight
  standard colors, or a psychedelic **neon rainbow** that cycles as
  it plays. Name and coat can be changed free at the shop any time.
  Buddies fight at your side and earn XP from their kills and your
  assists — generously, and training is cheap. A buddy left
  unbitten for a few seconds also catches its breath and heals.
- **EDDIE MODE** (title screen): every bug becomes a fuzzy orange
  puffball with a very big red nose. Pure cosmetics — same bugs,
  same fight, much more huggable. Records still count.
  No ads, no accounts, no nonsense — it's a premium game.
  All game text uses Atkinson Hyperlegible — a typeface designed
  for low-vision readers — drawn bare (no drop shadow, no outline)
  with the letters sized large in their cells, for easy reading on
  any background. The buddies wear glasses to match yours.
  Buddies never die: at 0 HP they **faint**, and the shop revives
  them for a price based on the buddy itself — its species, its
  training and its taught moves (a fresh pet rock wakes for 22 kg;
  a trained moose costs more). A faint never costs a buddy anything
  it earned: training, XP and moves all ride through.
- **Bug Bowl:** the mall's bowling alley. Every ball costs **one
  carcass off your head**. Position with left/right, then press FIRE
  three times — once to start the aim, once to lock the angle, once to
  throw. The scoring is real bowling: strikes and spares score with
  your following balls, and the tenth frame can run to three balls.
  Your best game is kept on the title screen.
- **Walls link up:** carcass walls placed side by side interlock into
  one run with a shared health pool (1.1× the sum). No pixel-perfect
  shuffling needed: a wall placed *near* a run **snaps into line**
  with it, end to end or around a clean corner. And building right
  **on top** of an existing wall stacks it — that spot just gets
  twice as much wall for the bugs to chew through. Runs of 5 or more
  get railings, straps and trim as they grow — and you can **climb up
  and walk along the top**, where bugs can't bite you (they'll chew
  the wall instead). One rule: a wall may never close into a complete
  loop. The loop must stay open.
- **Big hats are fragile:** once you're carrying 50 kg or more, every
  bite shakes a carcass loose. Haul the heavy hats home — or bank them.
- **The music is Beethoven now:** the run starts serene — the
  Moonlight Sonata's first movement — and the worse the THREAT gets,
  the fiercer the music climbs: Für Elise, then the Fifth Symphony
  (fate knocking), then the Moonlight's stormy finale. Zen mode stays
  with the Moonlight, calmly.

## Tips

- Your hat is your wallet: squashed bugs stack on your head and you pay
  by weight at the Bug Bounty store. The farther from the Epicenter you
  roam, the bigger (and heavier) the bugs.
- While anybody is inside the Bug Mall, the Epicenter shield is up
  and bugs can't reach the plaza.
- Out of health? The store sells a HOTFIX that heals 50 HP.
