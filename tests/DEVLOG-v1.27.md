# v1.27 — TOUCH & GO

Every hand that plays this game should be able to do everything the game has. This build is the one where the touchscreen stops being a second-class citizen — and a few old friends get long-overdue presents.

**THE SOURCE ROOM, UNSQUASHED:** The terminal in the Source Room used to amputate long lines of code at the edge of the screen, which is a poor way to read anybody's source, let alone your own. Now the text wraps, the way text is supposed to: long lines fold gently onto the next row, the cursor knows exactly where it lives among the folds, and scrolling moves through the wrapped page without losing your place. Nothing about your files changed — not one byte — only the reading of them. The whole terminal was audited while we were in there: hints, headers, the build log, the tuning screen, all of it fits its panel now, wide screen or narrow.

**THE SOURCE ROOM, BY TOUCH:** And the terminal is no longer view-only for fingers. On a touchscreen you can do the whole job: drag to scroll the file list and the code, tap a file open, tap to place the cursor, and type on the on-screen keyboard that rises when you start editing — spaces and all. A footer of big honest buttons covers the essentials: SAVE, NEW LINE, TUNE, BACK, so saving your work never depends on a keyboard shortcut a phone doesn't have. The prompt and tuning screens take taps now too, row by row, same as everywhere else in the game. Mobile or keyboard, the source is yours to modify.

**WAR! ROLLS THE DICE HARDER:** The battlefields have learned new tricks. Resource nodes wander from their old surveyed spots and carry a little more or a little less than the chart says. The debris fields come down thicker or thinner, in different tempers — some battles are woods, some are ruins. The great barrier lines still march across the field, but now some of them march sideways, and the hives sit further into their mischief than they used to, with an extra guard bug when the roll feels mean. No two fields alike — but the ground around your Compiler Core stays honest, and a battle you park is the battle you come back to.

**WAR! HOUSEKEEPING:** Two repairs. The WAR! cabinet in the Arcade sat too low in its room, like a picture hung by somebody sitting down — it has been raised to a proper height, and the room's south wall moved up with it. And on touchscreens, the SKIRMISH button on the war map could not be tapped: the spot it sits on was still claimed by invisible buttons from the main game's touch controls, which swallowed every tap whole. Those claims no longer apply inside WAR!, where no such buttons are drawn — SKIRMISH, and everything else on the war map and the setup panel, now answers a fingertip like it should.

**SUPER BUDDY RUN, LONGER LEGS:** The runner's jump was coming up short — the highest platforms, the ones floating over the pits, asked for more air than a standing jump had in it. The jump has been re-figured from the tiles up: the tallest rise in the game now clears with room to spare, and the short hop you get from tapping the button stays short. No platform moved. The legs just grew.

**TANK RESCUE, SUPPLY DROP:** Two new pickups roll out with the tank. The MEDKIT — a white box with a red cross you can read at speed — puts fifty points of hull back on the spot, and there is always one waiting a short drive from where you start, because a struggling driver should meet help early. Patrol wrecks drop them too, sometimes. And the GRENADE LAUNCHER: pick it up and the grenade pouch stops being a pouch. For the rest of the run, grenades are endless and lob faster, the readout says so in plain words, and the launcher stays with you across levels until the run itself ends. The fortress has been warned.

**SUPER BUDDY CART, NEW PAINT:** The Grand Prix got a dressing crew. Barrier posts and flowers along the verges, grandstands with roofs and stripes, a chequered start band two rows deep under a trimmed gantry, marker boards flanking the line, karts with racing stripes, number roundels and taillights, and a hazy far ridge behind the hills so the world has somewhere to be distant in. Same tracks, same physics, same medals — just prettier to lose on.

**CART MEDALS, PODIUM ONLY:** One correction from the stewards' office. Cart medals used to be a pure time trial — run a fast clock and the medal was yours, even if the whole field beat you to the line. That is not how medals work. From now on a medal has to be earned at the finish: first place can take gold, second can take silver, third can take bronze, and fourth or lower takes home a nice time and nothing else, however fast it was. The target times haven't moved — a slow win still won't buy the top step — but no podium, no medal, in single races and the Grand Prix alike. Medals already in the Trophy Case under the old rules stay there; they were earned fair and square by the lights of their day.
