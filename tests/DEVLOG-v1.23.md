# v1.23 — FRESH TUNES, LONGER ROADS

Two of the oldest parts of the game just grew up: the band learned new arrangements, and the Grand Prix learned what "grand" means.

**THE TUNES:** Every piece in the game has been re-arranged — same public-domain masterpieces in the same places, played like the band has been rehearsing. The Moonlight Sonata's triplets turn over in the dark and sink an octave before the loop; Für Elise's Alberti figures run top-down now, with little octave pings in the bass; Symphony No. 5's famous stabs answer DOWNWARD, because fate, frankly, has it coming. Moonlight III's storm sinks into the cellar for its second half while the bass starts pulsing on the fifth. Sheep May Safely Graze keeps every flute note Bach wrote, but the continuo player woke up — the throbbing bass pulse bounces to the octave now, the way a real continuo hand can't resist doing. The Mountain King creeps twice as fast in the bass once he's spotted you. The bumblebee's band drops out entirely for the two great falls and lets the bee scream alone, over a bass that crawls down the chromatic stairs after it. Greensleeves lilts. The Galop gets octave pings and a button on the phrase. William Tell's middle phrases answer from an octave down, and the Valkyries' horn calls echo in their own rests. Nothing got longer, nothing got shorter, and nobody's ears will bleed: every piece was re-measured note by note and sits within a breath of its old loudness.

**THE ROADS:** Super Buddy Cart's Grand Prix is a progression now, the way it always should have been. The Bug Meadows oval is untouched — same beginner loop, same first race. But every regional circuit beyond it has been stretched, folded and re-laid in a bigger country: Spaghetti Fields runs about 15% longer, Legacy Lands 30%, the Kernel Badlands half again, the Frozen Cache Fields 70% more, Neon RAM Flats nearly double, the Cable Jungle more than double — and The Unmaintained Depths, the Far Cup's finale, is a 2.4-times-the-Meadows monster with a final race distance to match. The far country fights back, too: the road narrows as you climb (30 wide at home, 22 in the Depths), the boost pads multiply to feed the longer straights, and from the Frozen Cache Fields onward there are tire stacks parked ON the racing line — clip one and you'll know. The rubber band stretches with the circuit, the minimap knows the new geography, and both Grand Prix cups inherit the whole ramp automatically: Home Cup is Meadows to Badlands, Far Cup is Cache to the Depths, and the Depths now earn the name.

Same karts. Same buddies. Longer roads, fresher tunes.
