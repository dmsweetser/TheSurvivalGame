# v1.16 — STICKS AND STRINGS

WAR! just learned to take orders from a gamepad, and every cabinet in the Arcade got its own orchestra.

**WAR! ON A PAD.** Park the mouse. A new cursor answers to the left stick — nudge it and a crosshair wakes up over the battle; from there the pad does everything a finger can. The A button *is* a tap: press once to pick a buddy, twice in quick step to muster every buddy of that kind on screen, three times to call up the whole army (harvesters sit that one out — somebody has to keep the bits coming). A on open ground marches the selection there. LB and RB walk a highlight along the command bar, A presses the lit button — train, build, done. Arming a building paints its ghost at the cursor, and B peels back one layer at a time: placement, highlight, selection. The right stick pans the camera with the cursor pinned to its spot on the screen, and if you fly the cursor into the edge of the view the camera follows like it means it. Keyboard, mouse and touch play exactly as they always did — last device touched wins.

**ONE TUNE PER CABINET.** The Arcade used to share one Bach aria. Now each cabinet plays its own:

- **ENTRANCES** creeps to Grieg's *In the Hall of the Mountain King* — quietly at first. It always starts quietly.
- **SUPER BUDDY RUN** gets Rimsky-Korsakov's *Flight of the Bumblebee*, which is only fair: the bugs unionised and demanded representation.
- **TANK RESCUE** rolls out to Offenbach's *Infernal Galop* — yes, the can-can. No, the tanks do not dance. Probably.
- **CASTLE** wanders to *Greensleeves*, all candlelight and old stone.
- **SUPER BUDDY CART** takes the *William Tell* finale — hi-ho, buddy, away.
- **WAR!** sounds Wagner's *Ride of the Valkyries* over the bit fields. The harvesters have requested smaller speakers.
- **BUDDY CRAFT** keeps *Sheep May Safely Graze*, because some things are already perfect.

The mall above still runs on Beethoven, and Banzai World's plaza keeps its Sheep. Every new arrangement is transcribed from the public-domain originals, note for note, in the same three-voice synth that's been humming since the first build.
